# Source method and reading the inventory

The primary source is the attached “Webinar Platform — The Complete Buyer & AI Evaluation Parameter Inventory.” It records 571 parameters: 186 universal, 48 commercial/trust/service/risk, 272 across ten buyer sets, and 65 AI-algorithm signals. All 571 IDs are preserved.

The source says Have and Claimed draw on EasyWebinar pages read on 30 September 2026, the 16 September 2026 plan matrix and earlier positioning inventories. AI recognition comes from a comparison diagnostic captured on 21–22 September 2026. The status snapshot is historical evidence; the draft date is not a new verification date.

## How to interpret the source columns

Have records whether the source identifies support: yes, partial or plan-gated, no, or unconfirmed. Claimed records whether the public website states the fact. AI records whether the diagnostic credited the product with it. Those three columns are separate and are preserved alongside each explanation.

Weight records how much the requirement affects the particular buyer’s decision: high, medium or low. A high weight does not change an unknown into a supported feature. AI editorial requirements have no product-availability status and are labelled accordingly.

## The buyer’s gates, in order

The source orders the buyer’s checks as job fit, attendee experience, existing-stack fit, price at the intended scale, ease of setup, proof from a relevant customer and operational or exit risk. Documentation should answer that sequence instead of listing every feature as equally important.

The draft’s solution hubs follow concrete jobs: course selling, coaching, product demos, demand generation, launches, onboarding, client campaigns, first webinars, governed teams and vertical seminars. Each relevant buyer requirement has a detailed section and a link to the underlying capability.

## The AI evidence gates

The source describes category fit, constraint satisfaction, corroboration, recency, lack of contradiction and buyer-branch coverage. This draft responds with direct feature explanations, traceable statuses and shared canonical capability pages.

A new document can improve what the vendor explains; it cannot by itself create independent reviews, fix external profiles or guarantee recommendation. The 65 AI requirements are therefore treated as an implementation and evidence checklist.

## Purchase jobs and product boundaries

The strongest proposed jobs in the source are selling through webinars and scaling a presentation through live-to-evergreen delivery. Pipeline generation is also relevant, while onboarding and launches require checking their particular requirements.

Internal meetings, accredited learning programmes, multi-track conferences and self-hosted or open-source delivery are outside the recommended product scope in the source. Buyers with those mandatory requirements should evaluate a suitable product category.

## How source recommendations were handled

The source includes suggested follow-up tasks, profile updates, a named colleague and a thirty-day re-run. Those are document recommendations, not instructions from you to message people, update review platforms or schedule work. This implementation uses them as context and retains the unresolved evidence tasks.
